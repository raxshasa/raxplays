I loved XCOM and it is to my great shame that I was never very good at it. Time to try a Stars Wars flavoured one.

## But first, the bad

Going to start with something that has annoyed me no end: performance. From what I can tell, the console version of this title is in decent enough nick. My suspicion is that it was in fact developed for console first, with the PC version being effectively a port. There are design decisions that back that up. 

The game looks nice enough, don't get me wrong. But there's not _that much_ that needs to be loaded in to memory. 

![Environments etc look nice enough, but there's not that much of it loaded in at any given time](src/assets/images/zero-company/tactical.jpg)
_Environments etc look nice enough, but there's not that much of it loaded in at any given time_

And despite that, I get my graphics card fans spinning at full pelt, juddering, inconsistent frame times and more, all at pretty low graphics settings. I'm really hoping that some of this is addressed in a patch, but given developer Bit Reactor seem to have laid off or furloughed a whole bunch of folks, it's hard to be sure _what_ is going on. It's a shame.

## Compelling decisions

Because what's on offer here is very compelling. XCOM is a wonderful game, but it has some issues. I feel that Zero Company addressed many of those. 

Generally, the format of things is very similar. You choose a squad of four operators and go off on missions. In between, you upgrade your people and your base. You get choices to make in the form of branching options, some of which might be mutually exclusive. There's a general sense of urgency, based on timers. So far so similar. But there are some key differences. 

First off, the assist system is _great_. This is a system where someone in the squad is able to call on one of their team mates to help out with a Bud Guy. 

![Pew pew](src/assets/images/zero-company/target.jpg)
_Pew pew_

Once your original operators takes a shot, the assisting operator will follow up with one of their own _for free_. In a game based purely on an action economy, this is a huge benefit to take advantage of. It leads to you setting up certain operators in certain places in order to make the most of the assist. 

And there are abilities that enhance this. My own primary character is based on boosting assist damage across the team, as well as their own. It ensures you lean in to buddying up your people. 

In turn, this benefits the Bond system, where your operators will be friends with each other (or not). This is one part of the wider meta section of the game, which I'll cover another time, which is... more mixed.

![I have mixed feelings about the sections in your base, which I'll cover another time](src/assets/images/zero-company/the-den.jpg)
_I have mixed feelings about the sections in your base, which I'll cover another time_

In general though, the systems involved within combat lead to some interesting decisions and compelling progress. There's a lot to love there.
