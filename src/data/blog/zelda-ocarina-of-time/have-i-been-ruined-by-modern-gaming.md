I'm not sure I feel right about this but, not to bury the lede, I'm reducing my rating on Zelda. Kind of can't believe I'm writing that. I'm still loving the game - 8.5 is still a tasty rating - but there's something that makes me feel 9.0 is just too high.

## Spoilt old man

I'm concerned that it's me, rather than the game. When fighting in dungeons I generally like how things are going. That's not the issue. And the graphics or audio fidelity are non-issues, as the design in both bases is sublime.

![You really don't see things like this any more](src/assets/images/ocarina/great-fairy.jpg)
_You really don't see things like this any more_

There are two things that are standing out to me really, the traversal and the signposting.

### A lack of direction

The very earliest stages of the game I powered through based mostly on memory. Since finishing Death Mountain, I'm relying more on finding my way as if I was playing for the first time. I'm tryihg to make sure I don't look anything up, as that would have been true in 1998, too!

Zelda requires some backtracking. That's fine. However some of that backtracking is caused by simply not knowing what to do next. The game does require you to speak to some of the locals to find out that to do next, but there are occasions where that stops. For example feeding a fish to a bigger fish to get it to open its mouth. I worked it out in the end, but none of the locals told me this.

I'm not asking for yellow paint daubed on ledges I can reach. What I'm hoping for is being able to solve environmental puzzles in logical ways that I can figure out. There are occasions where it doesn't feel like Zelda is giving me that. My most recent example was that I didn't speak to a certain character _enough times_ to get her to trigger something. It's not entirely intuitive. The signposting is exacerbated by the movement.

### Movement misgivings

Hyrule Field was a bit of a revelation at the time, in terms of its scale. It still feels that way, although it does now look at little sparse. Generally though, I like Hyrule. My issues come with the fact that the above complaint about a lack of signposting makes the running back and forth across Hyrule Field quite tedious.

There is no fast travel at this point in the game and that is very noticable. Indeed, when firing up the game each time you start in Kokiri Forest, meaning there's an enforced run every time. When you don't know exactly what you're doing on where you're going, this is an especially painful process.

## Dungeon diving

I feel odd marking down a game like Zelda. When it's good, it's _great_. The dungeon diving, the way they are laid out, the boss fights. All fantastic. The storytelling in-between is also excellent. Much of the game is brilliantly put together and the techniques used to overcome technical limitations are innovative and well chosen.

And I'm still loving my time with it. Perhaps this was just a frustrating session. It's not unlikely I'll be back to where we started after the next one!
