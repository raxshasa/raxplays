Ah Satisfactory. How do I love thee? Let me count the ways. To set the scene, I bought this in Early Access and thoroughly enjoyed it. Around update 8 I decided to wait for the full release. When 1.0 launched, it consumed me entirely. Rarely has aa game been designed more in service to quality of life and boy do I love it. For the avoidance of doubt, I think that Satisfactory is, for me, one of the best games ever made.

## Lack of willpower

Another update for Satisfactory is due before the summer. Patch 1.2 promises to be a decent one and include new things like fluid trucks etc. This is something I was very interested in and, frankly, any excuse to get back into it is fine by me. However, my willpower was not sufficient to stop me getting stuck into it early. It'll be ages until I get around to needing any new content anyway.

I'm not going to be able to cover all of the reasons I love this title in one hit, so I'll mention the one that is most apparant: speed.

## Fast feet

Everything about the game feels snappy. I've remapped a few controls to mouse thumb buttons, but generally build menus are fast, plopping things is fast, moving around is fast, running power is fast. All of these things take up brain power and cost you cognitive load, but as you become familiar with the systems, that cost diminishes. You're making the same decisions, but you're making them that much faster until they are almost second nature. Your first miner > smelter > contrustor flow will take you a little bit of time, but then it's faster every time.

This applies to basically everything in the game. From how you engage with the fauna to how to organise your belts, it's all stuff that becomes slicker and more optimal every time you do it.

I found this to be true when starting again, even with over 250 hours in the bank. I had forgotten _enough_ in the time since I finished it that there was some getting back up to speed. But boy was it fast.

## Next up

I can see myself getting back into this, so expect some updates on the nuances of throughput and merging mismatched item counts.
