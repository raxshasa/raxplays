I've hit one of Satisfactory's progression gates, so It's time to get serious about steel and making lots more of it than I have now. These gates happen when you finish up your tiers available at each project level. They stop you from unlocking much more, outside of smaller improvements in the MAM.

## Project parts and tiers

This wasn't a particularly long session, nor particularly focussed, as I wasn't feeling great. I still managed to progress towards project parts, however.

The way this game handles progress is quite interesting. It feels like there are loads of things to aim at, and there are. There are research nodes in the MAM, there are tiers that need certain numbers of materials to unlock and there is the over "project assembly" storyline, which is the main progress mechanism.

The MAM is the simplest. Some relatively simple requirements and you get new stuff. Mostly, this is upgrades to things you already have, like pwoer poles that can handle more connections or additional things you can search for with your scanner. It's also where certain larger items, such as the dimension depots I keep wanging on about, come from.

Next up are the tiers. There are 9 all together and _roughly_ two per project phase. These are more substantial sets of things, such as unlocking vehicles or entire new materials to use in manufacturing. It also includes the fundamental machines you need to manufacture more complex goods automatically.

Finally, there are the project phases. These drive the story, which is _relatively_ loose and require sometimes quite large volumes of things that are specific to the project. They serve no other purpose, which makes it easy to see them as "waste". For myself, this leads to me not starting on them initially, as I feel the need to get my house in order in other ways first. However, given you often need 1,000 or more of these items - and they can be as slow as kicking out 10 per minute unless you really ramp things up - the earlier you start, the sooner they are done.

I've found that there is typically one item that I set up machines for and then hand feed and one item that needs full automation. Typically, the component parts you need for the project parts are things you need anyway. It just means you need to siphon some of them off. While in Early Access, I found some of these requirements incredibly frustrating, but since 1.0 many of the values were tuned and the result feels a lot more fun and balanced.

It's therefore quite an intuitive way of progressing. If you are "locked out" of certain items in the MAM, you probably need to progress a tier. If those are maxed out, you're at project level. As an example, I now need various resins and rubber to carry on, but I know those are in oil refining, which is in the next project phase. Therefore that's my goal.

Once you get into the swing of it, it all makes sense. Even the longer, drip-fed parts you need mean you are incentivised to go and explore while you wait for mother time to produce the items you need. Late game, some of these are incredibly time consuming and require stacks of power to boot, so it's a good habit to get in to.

The factories you build for these project parts aren't wasted. Many of the project parts require decent numbers of the earlier project parts to construct, so anything you place down is probably valuable.
