News Tower feels like a a trap: in a good way. It's inherently a simple looking game. You're looking at a cut-away 2D representation of a building. There are some maps and other boards to look at, but by and large, you're staring at your little animated staff wandering around the place.

By oh my. There's so much here. I feel like it's something of a designers dream, with so many different aspects to get into. Today I want to take a look at the core news loop.
![Your main view for the game is your news tower](src/assets/images/news-tower/tower-view.jpg)
_Your main view for the game is your, er, news tower_

## Good news day

The core loop of the game is finding stories, sending our reporters and then assembling your articles before laying them out and printing. If there were nothing else to the game, this alone is a satisfying loop.

The process of finding the news is fun. You effectively have a series of phone lines, telegraphers, who are searching the country for news. The more stations you have doing this, the more news you can triage. While something is active, that "line" is busy, so you can't find more. If you don't want a particular story as it doesn't match your focus, you can dismiss it. If you fancy the look of something, you can drag one of your reporters to it.

![Sourcing the news is a case of triaging these icons](src/assets/images/news-tower/news-finder.jpg)
_Sourcing the news is a case of triaging these icons_

Reporters all have specialties, three each (that I've seen so far). They have varying levels of skill in these and there are six types of news, so you'll need a spread of folk to chase down stories.

Stories themselves can be simple one-and-done affairs or they can be branching, forcing you to make a choice, or in fact have multiple paths you can follow to flesh things out. You have to report on and then typeset your news within the week, so time is a factor.

The production pipeline requires a couple of different folks - typesetters and assemblers - and I've just unlocked the ability to add a copy editor to the front of that process. I can see that there are more things that could go alongside this, too. I'm not there yet.

You then pop the paper together on Sunday, ready to print. You have three articles on a page and I'm locked to two pages at the moment, but it clearly goes up to at least 5 or 6. Pull the lever once your done and off the stories go to the printer. Very satisfying.

![Pulling the lever takes longer and longer as your decision space gets more crowded](src/assets/images/news-tower/printing-time.jpg)
_Pulling the lever takes longer and longer as your decision space gets more crowded_

All stories have one of more icons that denote its type, so "crime" or "society", as well as other things such as if it's "educational" or "gossip". Balancing these icons - sometimes wanting to mix them, sometimes wanting to find combos - is what makes up a big part of your editorial decisions.

And they _do_ feel like editorial decisions. The stories themselves, fictional though they are, _feel_ real and make sense. It's fun that this mechanic makes you feel like you're an actual editor and the content backs up the emotional link to that.

## Extra spice

As I noted, this loop alone would be fun and evocative. But there's so much more to it. There's additional spicy little extras that make all of those decisions more interesting. Initially it's a case of printing any old icons and looking for matching ones. You then unlock the "informational" (roughly left wing) and "sensational" (roughly right wing) tags, which nudge your leaning one way or the other. Some people want to buy one of those types. They have certain things they want to see in their news. Advertisers come onto the scene. They want you to be consistent in the type of news you print in order to give you adverts you can run.

The more you print a certain type of paper, the more you learn about the people buying it and the more you can lean in to that. Some months that means taking a hit on the tracker that gives rewards or risks based on _how_ you're presenting the news. You start to make even more editorial decisions, removing aspects of some stories to tailor them to your audiience.

It's _fantastic_. The features I mention above are introduced relatively slowly, and earning money is also quite slow. But it's all connected and layered up like a particluarly delicious newsy cake.
