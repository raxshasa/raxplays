I started the session playing Going Medieval again before popping into Cyberpunk. I have mixed feelings on the former.

## Going Medieval

I felt OK about the game at lunch and I suppose that's still true. It's nice enough to look at: the simple aesthetic is generally pleasing and the lighting is surprisingly good. I just am finding it very slow in the early game. There are some titles, [like Manor Lords](../games/manor-lords), where the pace of the game is one of the appeals. In that title I don't often speed up time and yet it doesn't feel plodding. However in Going Medieval, I've spent most of the time with the game speed at max and it _still_ feels a bit plodding.

![I don't for a second believe static shots do this title much justice](src/assets/images/going-medieval/early-towns.jpg)
_I don't for a second believe static shots do this title much justice_

There are elements I enjoyed playing around with, like the cosmetic variations you can instantaneously switch, but otherwise I got a little bored and moved on.

## Phantom Liberty

When it comes to Cyberpunk, [I mentioned previously that I was feeling a little like I was slogging my way to the end game](end-of-game-trawling). Getting back into the Phantom Liberty content has changed that. One of the light criticisms I've levelled at the story generally is that there are so many branches and twists and characters that it's hard to follow it all coherently. That doesn't feel like the case in Phantom Liberty. It's self-contained and a lot sharper in terms of its use of characters.

![A new person I've met that gives me an excuse to show off another environment](src/assets/images/cyberpunk/dogtown.jpg)
_A new person I've met that gives me an excuse to show off another environment_

It's a hugely welcome change and I think it's one that will carry me to the proper end game. I plan on wrapping up the content here, the storyline with Judy and then getting stuck into the final stages. I am continually blown away with just how lovely the game can look and some of the interesting mechanics it throws into the mix, but I'm getting to the point where I'm almost a god in terms of how I can move and fight. I'm so elusive now that I only have to disappear for a second and all guards or hostiles lose me completely. That's without the active-camo I can activate. In one emergent opportunity, I activated my camo, ran 100 metres past all of the guards and bagged what I needed. No challenge in sight.

![This is tottally unrelated, it's just one of my favourite interiors](src/assets/images/cyberpunk/afterlife.jpg)
_This is tottally unrelated, it's just one of my favourite interiors_

I feel that now I've hit the kinds of heights Night City was promising from a character building perspective, it's probably time to ride off into the sunset. Or die or whatever is going to happen with the Relic.
