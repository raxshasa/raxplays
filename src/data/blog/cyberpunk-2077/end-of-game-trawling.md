At the end of longer games I tend to struggle with the amount of stuff to do I have open. I'm finding that now. Don't get me wrong, I'm enjoying the quests that I'm doing, but it does start to feel all a little disconnected.

## Tough solve

It's something that most open world games struggle with, all the way up to Baldur's Gate 3. While that title does an admirable job of things, especially towards the end, it's still true that games like this struggle to generate urgency without introducing timers, which are hateful.

And Cyberpunk suffers from the same. As I noted, I'm enjoying the content I'm playing. It's fun. But it's mostly in isolation: it's not often connected to the main storylines, if at all. And it doesn't have to be.

But because I want to see the content, I want to play it. But there's a lot of it stacking up at the end of the game. I know that starting the end game locks out all of the rest of the content permenantly.

I therefore need to hoover up all the things I want to do. But there's only so much of it I can do until I feel like I'm too far away from the main thread of the story. At that point I'll need to just go for it. Already, the storyline that I very much enjoyed with Panam seems a long way in the past: if she's going to appear in my endgame it's going to seem a little disconnected.

There's a silver lining here, which is Phantom Liberty. There's still content there that I've not got stuck in to and it feels significant. I'm almost deliberately slow playing that, waiting for the time to crack it open and finish things there.

## Next up

So while I'm enjoying the content, I'm dropping my overall rating of the game as it feels like I'm dragging my heels into the end game a little and I was hoping for an answer to this particular challenge.
