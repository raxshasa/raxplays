I've now rolled two endings. Here goes.

## The elephant in the room

It's something I noted previously, but it bears repeating that this is a title that launched in a bad state. And I didn't enjoy it at that point. Many of the positives as I see them now were there at launch: the narrative, the world building, the character design. All excellent. But it was a game that was hard to love.

It's definitely true that this impacted me in the early stages of my playthrough, but towards the end I'd pretty much forgotten all about it. There are still bugs in there, but they are far more "garden variety" and the sort of thing I would expect in something vaguely open world.

## Storytelling

I'm going to start with the bad. There are a _lot_ of names and a lot of threads. Like a _LOT_ a lot. I get it, it's a complex RPG, but when you are relatively time poor and some weeks are only able to hoover up an hour of game time here and there, it makes things hard to follow.

I'm not asking game design to pander to time-poor dads. I'm just pointing out that this is - for me - a tough thing to deal with.

The journal does a mostly OK job of making sure this is clear-ish, but I would have liked more of a glossary for keeping track of some of the people and kooks that you meet along the way, as well as highlights of my interactions with them.

Other than that, the stories are very strong. Side quests are part of the highlight here, as there are precious few that feel inconsequential or poorly thought through. Almost all of them have their own starring character or three and are multi-part affairs. River, a local cop, and a convicted felon are two seperate characters with fun arcs that stood out in their own right. It was only while writing this review that I really clocked they weren't on the main story path and both feel weighty and well written. 

These side options really feel like they had exactly the same amount of love given over to them as the main questline. I didn't finish up _everything_ there was on offer before finishing the game, but I don't feel like I left too much on the table, such is the strength of the content. For me, that is an uncommon thing. Jonny Silverhand, the main character in your head throughout the game, even chips in on many of these, offering opinion or suggestion. It's another thing that shows how well integrated it all is.

The main quest is also solid. As alluded to above, it's hard to seperate out every thread in my mind, but the overall premise of Jonny is excellent. The twists and turns you go through in your relationship with Silverhand are interesting, as you are able to play out various endings depending on how you decide you want to address things. What you think it fair. I'll come on to the Phantom Liberty content more generally, but the ending that that DLC offers is, in my opinion, much weaker than the base game option. I understand what it was doing - and the nature of it was very well done - but it was too deflating for me. Some people will love it.

In terms of base game content, pretty much everything after the point of no return is aboslutely fantastic. It'll depend on how you approach it, but there's something like 90 minutes to three hours of content in that final stint: I strongly recommend trying to get it done in one shot. 

## Visuals, animation and glorious characters

Yes, it's the area with the most bugs, but the characters of Cyberpunk are unreal in their execution. This is true of the models themselves and the way they are animated. Idris Elba in paricular, as part of the Phantom Liberty DLC, is outstanding. 

This is a game that trades on character relationships a lot, so this was something to absolutely nail down. Lots of love was clearly given over to the likes of Judy and Panam, and both add heaps of depth and warmth to the story. 

Even the general crowds in Night City look good, as long as you don't look at them too long. The aesthetic and style of most of the inhabitants lends them to making a crowd look visually interesting, while making it clear that this is a mob of NPCs you are looking at.

One negative I would point at here is the general lack of NPC population in a lot of the city. This is especially tue on the roads. Night City is big and the indivdual districts are significant. I suspect fitting those in memory was a massive challenge and that NPCs and vehicles added a lot of weight to the simulation. They necessarily had to be turned down, I suspect further than the creative design was strictly comfortable with. 

It does mean that this "impossibly crowded and busy city" doesn't often feel that way. In fact getting around by car is a dream.

And speaking of Night City: it's a wonderful thing to look at. The characters are full of life and expression and the city is no different. Everywhere you go there is a vista, or fantastic architecture, or visual interest. The lighting artists in particulat worked overtime on this one. It's stunning. I turned raytracing on for part of the game at the cost of framerate, as the way everything is connected by the light sources in play is sublime. I like raytracing generally, although am cautious about its use, but this should be a poster child for it.

## Phantom DLC

First off: this is one of the strongest pieces of additional content that I've ever played in any game. There's an image in the gallery above of a character leaning against a window they are looking out of. As someone who understands the context, I love that image. It's evocative of the style, tone, characterisation and story of the whole piece.

Also, a quick clarification: I said I didn't like the ending the DLC offered, and that's true, but by that I mean the literal ending you can choose once the bulk of the content is over. Prior to that is the end of the main story of the DLC, which is so compelling it's hard to properly explain. Again, there's a "point of no return" marker and once you hit that I strongly recommend finishing what you've started. It results in incredibly striking imagery as well as heavy hitting storytelling, including some of the most crunchy decisions I've found in a game for a long time.

Generally, the DLC brings narrative content and engaging characters by the dozen and then firehoses them at you. It doesn't help with the name and narrative overload that I've mentioned, but overall I found the Phantom Liberty gang to be a little more distinct and recognisable. 

It's not all roses, however. Dogtown, the area of the map that you get with the DLC, is not my favourite. I liked all interiors and characters and content, but the actual district felt more generic and uninteresting to me, with a couple of notable exceptions that are used for some of the main story beats.

Additionally, there are content drops that fall into the area and then some folks fight over them and I don't really know why? Perhaps it was explained to me? I don't know. If it was I missed it and this particular feature felt a little random and not very interesting to me. It's easily ignored, but a little jarring.

Overall, however, Phantom Liberty elevates the Cyperpunk experience significantly, taking it from a high-end "good" game into the realms of "great".

## Summary

Cyberpunk is not without its flaws, but many of those are born out of ambition and therefore feel easier to overlook. If you don't try and play this as a futuristic GTA, which was never the intent of the game, I suspect most people will have a most excellent time. It's one of the strongest RPGs I've ever played and would probably get a look in in my top 20 of all time. 

I didn't feel it at the time, but after finishing the game there's something vaguely Mass Effect-esque about it for me. It left me with a feeling that I haven't had much since that game: perhaps it's the narrative-laden, choice-driven nature where things feel like they actually _matter_. That's hugely important when creating something as rich and dense as _Cyberpunk: 2077_. And despite the rockiness of the launch, this title is a technical marvel. The performance I got on my modest rig with some light raytracing on and decent textures was admirable. 

All told, I can see myself playing through this again, which for a title of this size is no small testament to its world.
