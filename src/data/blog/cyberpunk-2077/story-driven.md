I'm moving through the story and, while there are some wrinkles, it's generally really well done if you squint at some of it.

There are some foibles, which I'll talk about, but on the whole the missions are fun and varied.

## The Good

In open world games or RPGs in general, there are often a lot of quests thrown at you. At their most basic, these can be fetch quests, kills quests or very basic investigations. You often spend time tracking back and forth between various locations.

![At least in Cyberpunk there's often not much traffic in the world...](src/assets/images/cyberpunk/night-drive.jpg)
_At least in Cyberpunk there's often not much traffic in the world..._

Cyberpunk handles this well. For a start, their quest design is pretty strong. Even if there are quests that are fetch / kill at their heart, there is an interesting narrative wrapper that elevates it. This means these quests hold your interests far longer, especially as they often branch off in unexpected and interesting ways. Or are oddball to begin with, in the case of needing to find AI taxis with divergent personalalities meaning they're doing weird things...

Additionally, [as I mentioned before](cyberpunk-is-back-on-the-menu), Night City is such a cool backdrop that the traversal is part of the appeal. In fact there are tools added to help you autopilot your way around in your vehicle if you like, to enjoy the city. I don't use the fast travel system and indeed never really even feel the need.

![Side missions often take you to some of the shadier areas of the city, which gives the game a chance to show off lighting](src/assets/images/cyberpunk/interior-lighting.jpg)
_Side missions often take you to some of the shadier areas of the city, which gives the game a chance to show off lighting_

As I'm now going through and chalking off some of the "side jobs" the game offers, I'm not finding any that _sound_ less appealing than others. When I know I'm near the end of a title I tend to feel the need to mainline the story in order to get it done. I then almost never clear up anything outstanding (and indeed can't in this game anyway). In Cyberpunk, I'm interested in actually completing the additional content offered, which is again a good sign.

## The Not So Good

It's not all roses, however. There are some janky aspects to the side missions.

First off is character interactions. There are lots of small, distracting things that crop up - often as a result of that open world - that can take away from the overall experience. In this session, during a relatively long, rolling chat with Jonny another character kept trying to call. The call would come in, the game would recognise that it can't take the call right now and V would give a "speak to you later" voice line. However, this character would then call again 15 seconds later. This went on or several minutes, resulting in around 10 calls. It's, seemingly, a _relatively_ simple thing to address that otherwise is very annoying in a generally relatively weighty conversation.

This is indicative of some of the bugginess that was rampant at launch and is, in general, much better. It's not completely gone though.

![Another image of a cityscape, just because I love them](src/assets/images/cyberpunk/cityscapes.jpg)
_Another image of a cityscape, just because I love them_

My other minor complaint is that it can be hard to follow. Never mind the sprawling nature of the main questlines, which are peppered with various names and characters, there are many, many more in the side missions.

When you're hopping between them, it can be overwhelming. I kind of just take it on faith that I've heard of some of the characters mentioned before and it's hard to retain all the names of people you're supposed to be friends with or mortal enemies with (outside of the main handful).

The quest log / journal does a fair job of managing a lot of this, but it's still a lot to take in. It's definitely best to see things through in these threads all the way to completion, rather than hopping around. But when the game isn't railroading you into doing that it's easy to feel the desire to branch out.

## Next up

I'm planning on getting through a few side quests and then going back to the Phantom Liberty content, which I believe I can and should complete prior to the endgame proper. Then it looks like I'll be on the home straight!
