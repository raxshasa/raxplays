Delving back into nostalgia with this shaky racing game that I find oddly compelling has been... interesting. Like many games on the N64, I predominantly played this with a friend and have very fond memories of it. I _think_ it was possible to run through the campaign tournaments in two-player, and it was awesome.

## Racing in the void

I think it's fair to say that it hasn't aged wonderfully. The races themselves are oddly devoid of much life. Such is the balance of things, you're either winning, and therefore can't see any of your rivals, or miles behind, where you can't see any of your rivals. It's very, very tough to be sat in the middle of the pack jostling for position.

This might be less of an issue if the tracks were particularly interesting. Draw distance generally on the N64 was an absolute disaster, but even with that the tracks are - on the whole - quite poorly realised.

There are a few exceptions to that rule: I like the Tatooine races. But otherwise you are often racing in the semi-gloom, by yourself.

It isn't helped by the music. When it's playing, in the menus and the pre-race stuff, it's great. However there are long period in the races where there is no audio other than the whine of your engines. That sounds good, but to the modern ear it's too stripped back and simple. There should be light music to punctuate the drama more often. It clearly exists, as it occasionally flares up, but it's so infrequent and seemingly random that it feels more like a bug.

## Despite myself, I find it charming

With that said, nostalgia is worth a lot and I quite enjoy the actual racing.

Partly, the boost system is to blame for that. It's a heady risk / reward balance that works well. Similarly, away from the track the upgrade system is well worked and both are things I don't feel that I've seen elsewhere. 

## Next up

In general, I intend to continue playing Racer in spare moment, if only to get Dud Bolt back to where he belongs: the very top.
