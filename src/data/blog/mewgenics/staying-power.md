I like my roguelikes to have pretty short feedback loops, as well as pretty consistent loops. Vampire Survivors literally having a 30 minute timer was ideal. Mewgenics does not have that.

Despite this, however, my early concerns that run length would be too long are unfounded. It's not because they aren't long - they are - it's because it's accessible. 

## Pick up and play

My concern with longer sessions in roguelikes is mostly around remembering things. I tend to hop between various devices and various games and I therefore have to work pretty hard to retain context. Longer runs with dense information make this difficult. It's something that I [experience a little with Darkest Dungeon 2](../posts/boss-fights-and-inventory-blight), where trying to retain deatils of the moveset of various characters or the synergies I was building can be tough. 

That's not the same in Mewgenics. Part of it is the presentation layer, which makes most of the information you need during a run far more accessible. The clear classes make this easier, too. 

The node map, the length of the battles, the simple inventory. All of these things help to raise the context of what you were up to last time you picked up the game. 

It's refreshing, as it means I can come back to a run and understand my place in it quickly. Even though a run might take a couple of hours - and I can see that getting longer and longer - I'm pretty confident that I'm not going to "lose my place". I'm also starting to see how the early stages of a run become more and more trivial, as I find "game sense" ways of beating certain enemies faster and faster. 

## Next up

This was just a short session but I was able to pack a lot in, which is again testament to how fast you can get through mini-milestones in this game. Looking forward to unlocking more options in the meta.
