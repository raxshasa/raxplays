I mentioned before that the pure aethetics of Mewgenics were not my favourite, although they are growing on me. But my word, the builds! Roguelikes often live or die on how they build through a run and Mewgenics looks like it might be near best in class in this regard.

## Builds and archetypes

There's a lovely stage in the pre-run setup in Mewgenics where you are tasked with assigning collars to your kitties. These collars determine their roles, which give buffs and nerfs to various stats: a "tank" will have increased health but be slower, a "fighter" has more strength etc.

You start with four and I've unlocked two more. It appears there are a few more to come. It's a very engaging way to ensure you check out the raw stats of your kitties, as well as their passive abilities, to ensure you are making optimal choices. No point making you fastest, low health moggy your tank as they aren't going to do a good job of it. It means you get to know your party a little better before heading out.

It also stops some of the early run malaise that you can find in other roguelikes. Rather than starting with a load of generalists and slowly finding boosts / items that tailor their skills, you are literally forced to pre-assign these roles and therefore think about it right at the top of the show.

This is a choice I like a lot. It's hampered a _little_ on Deck by feeling the need to then hover over every ability on every cat you're taking, which with the way the cursor works isn't delightful. On the whole, however, I love this setup and it serves to generate a much greater connection.

It helps that these archetypes are pretty well recognised. It's one area where the game seems to eschew silliness and fun language and instead uses recognised terms like "tank", "fighter", "cleric" etc. The starting one in particular are pretty common and the two I've picked up since are also a bit of a given. It's entirely possible that later collars are more esoteric, but by then systems are far better understood, so that's OK.

As a system, this makes in-run upgrades easier, too. You are more familiar with your cats and therefore able to pick up upgrades, which can also require some reading, in a far more logical manner. It makes a core part of the "engine building" of the run easy to engage with without adding huge cognitive load. Decisions are therefore based on the merits of what's in front of you.

Finding items throughout a run then adds to this process, as you can tinker and refine a little more.
