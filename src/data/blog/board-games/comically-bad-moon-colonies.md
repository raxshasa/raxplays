A couple of board games! Going to mostly talk about Bloodbath with a light touch on Marvel Unmatched. It's real good.

## Marvel first

Not going to spend long on this one. It's fine. The models are nice and the idea is cool. You can mix and match your sets to play off weird groups of heroes against one another. We played as a three, which is fine, although felt like it could lead to some "ganging up". One on one seems like it would be quite one-dimensional, however. It was simple enough to play and with kids 7 and up could be good fun. It's not something I'm going to rush out and buy.

## A proper bloodbath

So on to Bloodbath. This thing is great. At first glance it seems like there's absolutely loads going on: there's a lot of tokens for one thing and the volume of cards looks ominous. Once you get going, you realise how quick and sharp it actually is.

If someone knows the rules and you're playing with a group that is familiar with board game concepts generally, the teaching portion of this is about 5 mminutes. This is in part because you turn over an event deck that literally tells you the steps you're allowed to take, narrowing down the immediate set of decisions you can take. This is an engine building and - as the game notes - engine losing game. This allows you to build lovely, slick engines, lose them and pivot what you have left in another direction.

And everyone plays at once. This is inhernetly an insular game where players will be looking at their own tableau and cards, but the central event deck brings everyone together at the same time. You take great delight in talking about the combos that are popping off and the engine you're building.

Disasters help this immensely. The tone of the whole thing is incredibly satirical and disaster-oriented. It's five people dying during an airlock test and the flavour text hinting that the sentiment was that things were too crowded anyway. The whole thing is incredibly well written and the lovely artwork is nailed on in terms of theme.

The game moves along at a great pace. The event deck is ordered and so introduces the difficulty ramp in a measured and deliberate way. The rest of the game is random and you're able to build your engine in many different ways. Things **will** ramp up, too. Early on you're able to spin your engine often but this slows down as the "bad things" deck grows and you go more into disaster management. The game ends when the first person loses, and in the two games we played, it felt like all of us were within a handful of turns of going pop as the game ended. It's well balanced.

Although this leads to my main criticism of the game. As noted, you spend some time building an engine that is fun and engaging. The decision space is always broadly the same, but it's broad enough to be crunchy and you the player are the ones who determine what those decisions lead to. That aspect is fantastic. But as you lose the ability to trigger all the various aspects of your engine, some of the fun of putting it together is a little lost. Early on, it's interesting trying to piece things together and there's a roughly ten minute period where everyone is pulling the lever of their machine and getting lovely, dopamine enducing payouts. And then it starts to fall apart.

To be clear, I _like_ the falling apart, but it falls apart because you're no longer able to pull the lever as often as before and there's something mildly unsatisfying in that. This is a pretty minor complaint, the game sings, but it is something I'd love to think on in terms of other ways this could be addressed.

Generally though, this is a super game and one I'd probably pick up if I happened upon it in a shop somewhere.
