I wanted to watch more of Maul and Manor Lords therefore seemed like the thing that would scratch my itch. It is a _very_ chill game that feels a little bit like you're just pottering around.

## Pace

This is both a good thing and a bad thing. I enjoy the fact that you can play Manor Lords and not feel like you are rushing around doing a hundred things at once. No one moves quickly, it's hard to miss anything. 

But this can be frustrating, too. It's sometimes hard to debug why things are being built slowly or not at all. The answer is normally something to do with an ox, but it can be a pig to work that out.

![The environments are truly lovely and offer and make for a properly idyllic setting](src/assets/images/manor-lords/idyll.jpg)
_The environments are truly lovely and offer and make for a properly idyllic setting_

Builings go up slowly, especially as you tend to need to assign plenty of your precious families to the more interesting jobs other than building, which is the remit of the unassigned. I understand that this is part of the balance, but it can - and does - lead to a bit of micro work, where you need to actively manage the numbers of people doing each job.

This is exacerbated a little by the fact there's already some micro required for the changing seasons. You don't always need multiple families assigned to the farm and there's no point having fisherman when you have a frozen pond.

Generally, I'm aware this is something that I myself make worse by the way that I'm playing. I could fix these things more quickly than I do. I guess my issue with it is that the fixes I would need to do are changes in style that I don't really want to make.

![Visually, there is no better looking settlement builder than this](src/assets/images/manor-lords/drought-season.jpg)

## Next up

I didn't actually think I'd find myself playing this game much at the moment. It's still got some time required in Early Access in order to get all of the content in for the richer, more nuanced experience it could be. Aside from content, there's also some mechanical depth missing in systems such as spoilage. However, there's still something to this title that I find compelling, so it's entirely possible I'll play a little more on my Steam Deck.
