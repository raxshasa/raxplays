To be perfectly honest, I somewhat forgot that I'd bought Space Marine 2 when I picked up Jedi Surivor, but I'm looking forward to comparing the two.

The Star Wars and 40k universes are two of the biggest out there are the moment. Yes, Star Wars is _significantly_ larger in terms of mindshare, but I'd warrant that 40k isn't as far behind as you might imagine. It also makes a lot more consistent money than Star Wars does at the moment.

## Striking back

I've been thinking about picking up this title for a little while and there was enough of a discount on to make me press the button. I initially didn't enjoy the first game, but somewhere in the middle that opinion flipped and by the end I felt like it told an excellent Star Wars story. One of the best to come from video games, in fact.

And I should point out, I'm aware that these two titles aren't actually _that_ similar. They are both futuristic-y, alternate Sci-Fi universes. They are both third person. They are both broadly action-adventure games.

But Survivor is, naturally, far more melee focussed and bears more in common with Souls games than the shootier, actionier Space Marine game. In feel, they are quite different in the end.

## Continuing in meat head mode

While I waited for Jedi Survivor to download, I played a bit more of Space Marine. I'm still enjoying the title and the slightly maguffin nature of the plot. It's schlocky in the way I hoped it would be. I was, however, keen to get stuck into Survivor.

## Elegance of a blade

Part of the beauty of Outcast, the first Jedi game, was the slow discovery of your powers and unlocking of abilities. You progressed through various trees, ultimately ending up building your own lightsaber, which was very cool.

And the second games seems to... completely ignore that. It doesn't seem to matter than I ended that game as a bit of a badass, I still just seem to have a skill tree to unlock. Unless I somehow missed it, there doesn't seem to be a narrative hook for that. I suppose that's fine, it's nice and simple, but I was expecting a Mass Effect 2 style "you've been rebuilt" plot thing to explain the requirement.

Otherwise, it seems a lot like a tasty sucessor. The cutscenes maintain the same high production values as in the first title and the combat remains slick and satisfying, when I'm not being rubbish at it. It has been a while since I played the first title, but there feels like there's more platforming this time, and it's well executed. I feel like I'm a Jedi doing things only a Jedi could do and - really - that's the whole point.

## Next up

At the moment, I feel very "seven" about both of these titles: they are fun without being exceptional. Interesting enough without being revolutionary. A seven feels like my resting point for a lot of games that don't otherwise distinguish themselves. I'm interested to see how I fare with both of these and how things change.
