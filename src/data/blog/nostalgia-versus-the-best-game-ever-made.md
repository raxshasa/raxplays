Getting to play Ocarina of Time again was one of the reasons picking up the Analogue 3D really appealed to me. There's a whole load of N64 titles that I have a lot of love for, and Ocarina might well be at the top of the pile. Given there's are approximately 1,000,000 games in existance [citation needed], it's not likely that many titles have more than 1% of "My best game ever" awards, but I reckon Ocarina is one of the ones that gets the closest. However, how much of that is pure nostalgia?

![It's a properly lovely looking bit of hardware](src/assets/images/ocarina/ocarina-in-the-analogue.jpg)
_It's a properly lovely looking bit of hardware_

## Going up Death Mountain

I'm at the stage of the game where I'm still a kid and I'm early on, just starting things in Goron City, looking to get the Spiritual Stone there. At the start of my session I, obviously, wake up in Kokiri Forest... One of my few major gripes with the game is this. Unless you're in a dungeon you get punted back here and until you get warp songs, it's a right pain.

Once I correct all of this, I spend my time working through Goron City and knocking out most of the puzzles there. I don't remember much of the game, but there are areas that come back to me pretty quickly as I play. It's a weird feeling of vaguely knowing what to do, but having the specifics and the details elude you completely.

![I had completely forgotten about this guy](src/assets/images/ocarina/goron-big-man.jpg)
_I had completely forgotten about this guy_

Personally, I think there are plenty of areas where the game holds up wonderfully. The atomic elements of it are delightful: exploring dungeons, unlocking new things and working at something until you solve it, give up or decide you need some item you don't have yet is great. The game is pretty stark at making some of those clear, which adds to the sense of discovery.

It also does a great job of teeing up _enough_ of this information if you're willing to look for it. Simply speaking to the Gorons - something I feel is a bit of a lost art in modern titles - gives you all the information you need to make certain decisions. None of it is on-screen prompt, none of it is quest marker or a quest log style system. It's all environmental and diagetic.

And there's a genuine sense of wonder to it all. Firstly, the music does a lovely job of setting tone and ambiance, especially given the limited options available at the time. Second, doscovery is at the core here. As you kick open a new chest that has taken you several minutes to establish the route to, plus a couple of puzzles, the reward feels so, so worth it.

![If you know the game, I bet you're hearing the audio that accompanies each of these right now](src/assets/images/ocarina/ocarina-chest-open.jpg)
_If you know the game, I bet you're hearing the audio that accompanies each of these right now_

Obviously the textures are pretty muddy and there's a lot of tiling, but this is the N64 and Ocarina was a bang out at the time. Visually, it's still very appealing. A little brown in places, but the sense of scale is done incredibly well for the era and many of the characters have stacks of charm.

## Adventuring through the past

Generally, I'm loving taking the time to pick through this title. Getting to do this as an adult with all the context of how things have changed both in the industry and in life in general is very cool and, hoestly, a little odd. I look forward to further sessions, but suspect that Ocarina of Time is likely to remain as one of my favourite ever games and quite likely my favourite adventure game of all time.
